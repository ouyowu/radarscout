# RadarScout partner model inspired by Toristy-style travel distribution

## 1. Executive summary

RadarScout’s future partner model should extend the current Thailand experience discovery product without turning RadarScout into a booking engine or a Bókun clone.

RadarScout should position itself as an AI-guided Thailand experience discovery and handoff platform:

- Travelers use RadarScout to discover, compare, and choose trusted local experiences.
- RadarScout helps translate travel intent into product-fit recommendations, itinerary ideas, and safe booking handoff links.
- Bókun and other booking partners remain responsible for checkout, payment, live availability, inventory, booking modification, and confirmation.
- RadarScout owns discovery, recommendation, editorial trust, filtering, partner-facing demand generation, and safe handoff.

The near-term opportunity is to build a partner and supplier cooperation model around RadarScout’s strengths: AI-guided intent capture, curated Thailand inventory, content-led acquisition, and partner-direct handoff. The wrong move would be to rebuild a global activity marketplace, supplier extranet, agent portal, cart, checkout, or live inventory platform before demand and partner contracts justify that complexity.

## 2. What RadarScout can learn from Toristy-style platforms

Toristy-style B2B travel distribution platforms point toward useful concepts RadarScout can adapt carefully.

Useful concepts:

- Partner or travel-agent entry point: a B2B page that explains how hotels, travel agents, concierges, DMCs, and creators can use RadarScout to route travelers toward trusted Thailand experiences.
- Supplier onboarding: a clear path for local operators to submit experience details, public booking links, photos, trust signals, and fit criteria.
- Destination partner / DMC positioning: RadarScout can work with destination specialists who understand local inventory, traveler intent, and practical trip design.
- Curated inventory access: partner-facing pages can explain that RadarScout prioritizes fit, trust, and traveler context rather than acting as a generic unlimited marketplace.
- Filters by destination, category, duration, cost band, style, and traveler type: these are useful for discovery and recommendation, as long as they do not imply live inventory or confirmed prices.
- Commission transparency on B2B pages only: agent economics, referral terms, or commercial models may be discussed in private or B2B contexts, but not on tourist recommendation cards.
- White-label, API, and widget concepts as future-only ideas: these can be roadmap options after contracts, margins, content quality, and handoff stability are proven.

RadarScout should borrow the distribution logic, not the operational burden. The first version can be documentation, static interest pages, and manual workflows rather than login systems or automated supplier infrastructure.

## 3. What RadarScout should not copy

RadarScout should not copy the parts of B2B travel platforms that would expand it into a premature booking or inventory system.

Do not copy:

- A global activity marketplace clone.
- Tourist user registration at this stage.
- Agent portal at this stage.
- Cart, checkout, payment, or booking engine.
- Live availability or live inventory claims.
- Supplier net rate, partner rate, or commission wording on tourist pages.
- Bókun backend, Bókun database, Bókun-powered, or powered-by-Bókun wording on public tourist pages.
- Fake ratings, fake reviews, urgency claims, or guaranteed-slot language.
- Automated supplier sync, booking modification, cancellation, payment handling, or confirmation handling.

Tourist-facing pages should stay clean and traveler-centered. Use language like:

- trusted local experiences
- booking partner
- partner booking page
- secure handoff
- real local experiences
- owner-managed experiences
- compare before you continue

Avoid language like:

- supplier net rate
- partner rate
- commission
- Bókun database
- Bókun backend
- powered by Bókun
- live availability
- available now
- instant confirmation
- checkout
- payment

## 4. RadarScout three-layer model

### Tourist layer

Current and near-term tourist-facing product:

- AI-guided discovery.
- Chiang Mai Experience Finder.
- Real product recommendation.
- Public booking partner handoff.
- Safe preference filtering by traveler type, style, duration, and category.
- No checkout inside RadarScout.
- No payment inside RadarScout.
- No live availability or confirmed slot claims.
- No public display of commission, supplier net rate, partner rate, or internal catalog details.

The tourist layer should answer: “Which Thailand experience fits my trip?” It should not answer: “Is this exact slot available and can I pay here?”

### Partner layer

Future static pages:

```text
/partners
/suppliers
/destination-partners
```

Purpose:

- Collect inbound interest from travel agents, hotels, DMCs, local operators, creators, and destination partners.
- Explain how RadarScout matches travelers with Thailand experiences.
- Describe safe cooperation models without promising a portal, live inventory, or booking engine.
- Use contact or interest forms only.
- Avoid login, dashboards, account management, automated commissions, and booking controls until demand justifies them.

The partner layer should answer: “How can my business cooperate with RadarScout?” It should not become a full extranet.

### Operator layer

Future private workflow:

- Manual product intake.
- Public booking link collection.
- Product-fit tagging and traveler-style classification.
- Content and photo guidelines.
- Trust checklist.
- Booking handoff validation.
- Basic partner notes and internal review.
- No live inventory sync unless explicitly approved later.
- No pricing claims unless sourced from public-safe product data and explicitly reviewed.
- No direct booking management.

The operator layer can remain lightweight for a long time. A spreadsheet, reviewed doc, or private internal tool is safer than an early supplier portal.

- `Become a RadarScout partner`

- “RadarScout helps travelers find experiences that fit their trip style, then hands them off to the booking partner when they are ready.”

- “Partner terms are reviewed manually.”

Forbidden copy:

- Do not say “instant commission dashboard.”

- Do not say “book and pay inside RadarScout.”
- Do not mention supplier net rates or partner rates on tourist-visible surfaces.

Suggested form fields:

- Name.
- Company.
- Role: agent / hotel / creator / DMC / other.
- Website or social profile.
- Primary destination focus.
- Expected traveler type.
- Email.
- Message.

- Category.
- Duration.

## 7. Future roadmap

### Phase 1: Static partner and supplier interest pages

Goal:

- Publish basic B2B interest pages after copy review.
- Use contact forms only.
- Keep manual review.

Possible pages:

- `/partners`
- `/suppliers`
- `/destination-partners`

Do not add:

- Login.
- Dashboard.
- Commission calculator.
- Booking controls.
- Supplier API.

### Phase 2: Manual supplier/product intake workflow

Goal:

- Standardize operator intake.
- Collect public booking links, product facts, photos, and trust signals.
- Validate handoff URLs.
- Classify experiences for recommendation scoring.

Possible artifacts:

- Supplier intake checklist.
- Public booking URL validator.
- Content/photo guidelines.
- Trust and safety review template.

### Phase 3: Private partner dashboard after real demand

Goal:

- Build a minimal private workflow only if partners repeatedly need status, submissions, or lead tracking.

Possible capabilities:

- Submission status.
- Partner contact records.
- Manual lead notes.
- Reviewed product profile status.

Still avoid:

- Checkout.
- Payment.
- Live inventory.
- Booking modification.
- Automated commissions.

### Phase 4: Optional API/widget integrations after contracts and margins are clear

Goal:

- Evaluate whether partner widgets, white-label pages, or API-style handoff tools are worth building.

Requirements before this phase:

- Clear partner contracts.
- Margin or referral model understood.
- Safe tracking model.
- Legal review of claims and responsibilities.
- Explicit approval for any API or sync work.

### Phase 5: SEO/index rollout only after content depth and trust signals are ready

Goal:

- Move selected tourist or partner pages from private/noindex experiments into public growth channels.

Before index/follow:

- Add enough unique editorial copy.
- Add trust signals.
- Review claims and forbidden wording.
- Confirm canonical strategy.
- Confirm sitemap and navigation strategy.
- Confirm no placeholder, fake, or internal-only wording.

- [ ] No checkout or payment inside RadarScout.
- [ ] No cart.
- [ ] No live availability claims.
- [ ] No live inventory claims.
- [ ] No Bókun API calls without explicit approval.
- [ ] No Bókun sync without explicit approval.
- [ ] No Bókun backend, Bókun database, or powered-by-Bókun wording on tourist pages.
- [ ] No supplier net rate wording on tourist pages.
- [ ] No partner rate wording on tourist pages.
- [ ] No commission wording on tourist pages.
- [ ] No fake ratings or fake reviews.
- [ ] No “available now,” “live slots,” “guaranteed slot,” or “instant confirmation” language.
- [ ] No production deploy from documentation-only tasks.
- [ ] No supplier admin URLs, API keys, secrets, tokens, or passwords in forms or docs.
- [ ] Booking handoff URLs must be customer-facing public booking pages or embeddable widget URLs.
- [ ] Tourist-facing CTAs should stay simple, such as “Check availability” or “View experience details.”

## Summary recommendation

RadarScout should build a partner model as a lightweight cooperation layer around its core tourist experience:

```text
Traveler intent -> AI-guided discovery -> curated recommendation -> trusted booking partner handoff
```

The business model can evolve toward partners, suppliers, and destination partners, but only after the current discovery-and-handoff loop proves stable. The first safe step is static B2B interest pages and manual intake, not portals or booking infrastructure.
